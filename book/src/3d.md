# 3D: meshes, materials and the sun

The same engine draws 3D: the same ECS, systems, clock and tools, with
lit meshes under the 2D layer. Turn it on in `Cargo.toml`:

```toml
[dependencies]
crabbyengine = { version = "1", features = ["3d"] }
```

Games that leave it off pay nothing for it. The C library (`crabby_ffi`)
always has it.

A 3D entity has a `Transform3D` (where it is, which way it faces, its
size) and a `Model` (which mesh to draw, and a tint). Here's a village of

## Space

3D space is right-handed with +Y up. The default camera looks along -Z.
Units are yours to choose; meters work well. A `Transform3D` has a
`position`, a `rotation` (a quaternion, `Quat`) and a `scale`, and is
built the same way as a 2D one:

```rust
Transform3D::at(vec3(0.0, 1.0, -5.0))
    .rotated(Quat::from_rotation_y(0.5))
    .scaled(2.0)
```

`t.looking_at(target)` turns it to face a point, and `t.forward()` is the
way it faces (its -Z axis). C++ has `crabby::transform3d_at(position,
rotation, scale)`, `crabby::looking_at`, `crabby::forward`, and `*` on
quaternions and vectors.

## Meshes and materials

A **material** says how a surface looks: its `color` (and an optional
`texture`), how rough it is (0 is a mirror, 1 is chalk), how `metallic`
(0 for most things, 1 for bare metal) and the light it gives off
(`emissive`). Lighting is physically based, as in glTF, so models made in
Blender look the same here.

A **mesh** is triangles with materials. There are three ways to get one:

- **Shapes**: `MeshData::cube(size)`, `plane(size)` and
  `sphere(radius, segments)`, then `ctx.create_mesh(&data, material)`.
- **Your own triangles**: fill a `MeshData` with positions, indices (three
  per triangle, counter-clockwise from the front) and optionally normals
  and texture coordinates. Normals you leave out are computed.
  `create_mesh` checks that the data fits together.
- **glTF files**: `ctx.load_mesh("models/house.glb")` loads a `.glb` (or a
  `.gltf` with its files) from `assets/`, with every mesh in its scene, its
  materials and base color textures. Loading the same file again returns
  the same mesh.

Meshes and materials are cheap handles: make each one once, at startup,
and spawn as many models of it as you like. A `Model` tint
(`Model::new(mesh).with_color(c)`) multiplies the mesh's colors, so a
thousand cubes can come in a thousand colors from one mesh.

## Camera

`ctx.camera3d` is the 3D camera. It has a `position` and `rotation`, and
`look_at(target)` turns it to face a point. Its `fov_y` is the vertical
field of view in radians (60° by default), and `near` and `far` bound
what's drawn. `ctx.camera3d.screen_ray(mouse)` gives the ray under a
window point, for picking things with the mouse.

C++: `ctx.camera3d()` and `ctx.set_camera3d(c)` read and write it,
`ctx.camera3d_look_at(target)` turns it, and `ctx.screen_ray(point)`
returns the ray.

## Light and shadows

`ctx.lighting` holds the sun and the sky:

- `sun_direction`: which way sunlight travels. Straight down is noon;
  a low angle gives long shadows.
- `sun_color` and `sun_intensity`: the sun's color and brightness.
- `sky_color` and `ground_color`: soft light from above and below,
  scaled by `ambient_intensity`.
- `shadows` and `shadow_distance`: the sun casts soft shadows up to that
  far from the camera (60 units by default), fading out at the edge.

The shadow map follows the camera and moves in whole texels, so shadow
edges stay still while the camera moves.

## Mixing with 2D

3D draws first and everything 2D draws on top: sprites, shapes, text, UI
and the inspector. A 3D game can have a 2D HUD with no extra work.
`ctx.clear(color)` sets the background behind both.

## Cost

Models with the same mesh are drawn together in one instanced draw call.
Models outside the camera's view are skipped, except when they cast a
shadow into it. Edges are smoothed with 4x multisampling.

On an M4 Max MacBook Pro, 100,000 spinning cubes with shadows run at
402 FPS, fullscreen with vsync off: 2.5 ms of CPU a frame, in 28 draw
calls. The same scene in Unity 6, made of GameObjects, runs at 54 FPS
([the comparison](performance.md#3d-crabby-vs-unity)). `ctx.models_drawn()` counts the
models drawn last frame, and `ctx.render_stats()` gives the frame's draw
calls and CPU time.

## Not yet

These are on the way:

- 3D physics;
- skinned and animated models (glTF animation);
- point lights and spot lights;
- transparent materials (alpha below 0.5 is cut out, for leaves and
  fences);
- tried and tested 3D in the browser (it builds for the web, but no one
  has run it there yet).
